    The work

    Nobody had counted these floors. So we walked them.

    There was no register of Riyadh's independent car showrooms: no list of doors, no stock counts, no prices, no idea how buyers pay. Our field team built one from the pavement up, one doorway at a time.

    i.

    Map the doors

    Started from public map listings, then walked every street of both districts to pin each showroom at its actual door with phone GPS.

    ii.

    Floor survey

    A first visit records what can be seen: vehicle type, brands, showroom size, cars on the floor inside and outside, and the average price.

    iii.

    Sit-down deep dive

    A second visit with the owner covers monthly sales, financed sales, partner banks and buyers lost when financing fails.

    iv.

    Photograph and file

    Facades are photographed and filed by the name on the board. Every figure syncs to one shared roster in the field app.

      The finance gap

      SAR M a month walks out of 24 showrooms

      On sit-down visits, owners told us how many cars they sell a month, how many of those are financed, and how many ready buyers leave when financing falls through. Priced at each floor's average, the lost sales add up fast.

      Two markets

      One city, two very different car markets

      Al Qadisiyah, off Dammam Road in the east, is a dense block of new-car independents. Al Shifa, in the south, is a used-car strip. Each dot is a showroom we walked into, sized by the cars on its floor.

      The exotic fringe

      Rolls-Royce two doors from a SAR 70k lot

      Inside a mass-market district sits a real luxury segment. ALBARIMI, Al Buraimi's high-end floor on Wadi Ar Rimah, lines up Rolls-Royce, Lamborghini, McLaren and Brabus G-Class under one roof.
